Quantum Resonance Gravity — Overview

Quantum Resonance Gravity (QRG) is a resonance‑based framework in which gravity, electromagnetism, and particle structure arise from a single underlying field. Instead of treating gravity as geometric curvature, QRG interprets gravitational behaviour as variations in the local resonance frequency of a universal field.

The Core Idea

Matter and electromagnetic fields excite a resonance substrate. Spatial variations in the resulting resonance frequency produce gravitational effects, while stable resonance modes behave as particle‑like excitations. This provides a unified physical mechanism for:

  • gravitational time dilation
  • electromagnetic sourcing
  • mass generation
  • charge and gauge structure
  • spin‑like mode symmetries

The Trilogy

The QRG programme is presented in three papers:

  1. Paper I: Classical resonance dynamics and the origin of gravity.
  2. Paper II: Electromagnetic sourcing and field coupling.
  3. Paper III: Quantum structure and the emergence of particles.

Together, these papers establish a coherent foundation for a resonance‑based unified field theory, with future work exploring deeper unification and Standard Model structure.
