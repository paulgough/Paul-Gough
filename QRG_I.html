Abstract

Quantum Resonant Gravity (QRG) is formulated as an infrared scalar–tensor effective field theory in which a Goldstone-like scalar field $R(x)$ and its normalized gradient $\hat{R}_\mu$ define a dynamical preferred direction. Matter and light couple to distinct emergent metrics, producing a natural separation between gravitational and electromagnetic sectors. This first paper establishes the foundational structure of the theory, including the scalar action, the construction of the normalized gradient field, the emergent metrics, and the weak-field limit.

The framework reproduces Newtonian gravity, predicts a nonzero gravitational slip $(\Phi \neq \Psi)$, and provides the mathematical basis for the tensor sector developed in QRG II. The scalar potential is motivated using renormalization-group flow arguments, and the resulting structure constrains the nonlinear and cosmological dynamics explored in later papers of the series.

Key Results

  • Definition of the scalar field $R$ and the normalized gradient $\hat{R}_\mu$.
  • Construction of the matter and optical emergent metrics.
  • Derivation of the weak-field and post-Newtonian limits.
  • Prediction of a characteristic gravitational slip parameter.
  • Identification of the scalar potential structure from RG flow.
  • Establishment of the mathematical foundation for QRG II–VII.
