Summary

QRG VI establishes the theoretical consistency of the resonance framework by analysing stability, causality, and the infrared behaviour of the scalar–tensor system. The paper demonstrates that both the scalar and tensor modes are free of ghosts, tachyons, and gradient instabilities across the physically relevant parameter space.

The emergent matter and optical metrics are shown to define a consistent causal structure, with the scalar field enforcing a preferred direction without violating Lorentz covariance at the level of observable physics. The infrared limit of the theory is analysed in detail, demonstrating how QRG reduces to general relativity in high‑acceleration regimes while preserving its distinctive predictions at low accelerations.

These results provide the final theoretical foundation required for the cosmological development presented in QRG VII.

Key Results

  • Proof of stability for scalar and tensor modes (no ghosts, no tachyons).
  • Consistent causal structure defined by the matter and optical metrics.
  • Infrared limit recovers GR in high‑acceleration regimes.
  • Persistence of QRG signatures in low‑acceleration environments.
  • Constraints on the scalar potential and coupling parameters.
  • Final theoretical groundwork for cosmology in QRG VII.
