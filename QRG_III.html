Quantum Microphysics, Substrate Hamiltonian, and Excitations

Independent Researcher

Summary

QRG III develops the microscopic physics underlying the resonant substrate introduced in QRG I–II. The scalar field $R(x)$ is interpreted as a coarse‑grained descriptor of a deeper quantum medium, and this paper constructs a Hamiltonian framework capable of generating the resonance equation and its emergent scalar–tensor structure.

The analysis identifies the substrate as a correlated quantum system whose collective excitations reproduce the scalar and tensor modes of QRG. The coupling constant $g_R$ is shown to arise from the interaction strength between microscopic degrees of freedom, and the theory naturally yields Lorentz‑invariant excitations in the long‑wavelength limit without requiring geometric curvature at the fundamental level.

This microphysical foundation provides the bridge between the effective field theory of QRG I–II and the astrophysical and cosmological applications developed in QRG IV–VII.

Key Results

  • Construction of a microscopic Hamiltonian for the resonant substrate.
  • Derivation of the scalar resonance equation from collective quantum dynamics.
  • Interpretation of the coupling constant $g_R$ as a microphysical interaction strength.
  • Emergence of Lorentz‑invariant excitations without curvature.
  • Identification of scalar and tensor modes as collective excitations.
  • Microphysical grounding for the phenomenology explored in QRG IV–VII.
