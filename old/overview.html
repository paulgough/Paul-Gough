Quantum Resonance Gravity — Overview

Quantum Resonance Gravity (QRG) is a resonance-based framework in which gravitational phenomena arise from the collective dynamics of an underlying physical substrate. Instead of treating gravity as geometric curvature, QRG interprets gravitational behaviour as variations in the local resonance amplitude of a universal medium. Classical spacetime emerges as the long-wavelength, low-frequency limit of this dynamics.

While QRG I focuses on the scalar, weak-field sector, the full programme extends to tensor modes, quantum microphysics, and cosmology, forming a coherent resonance-based alternative to geometric gravity.

The Core Idea

Matter and fields excite a resonance substrate. Spatial and temporal variations in the resulting resonance field produce gravitational effects, while coherent excitation modes behave as particle-like or wave-like structures. This provides a unified physical mechanism for:

  • gravitational time dilation
  • Newtonian and relativistic limits
  • light bending via refractive-index variation
  • tensorial wave modes (QRG II)
  • quantum excitations and particle structure (QRG III)
  • cosmological evolution and large-scale dynamics (QRG IV)

The QRG Research Papers

QRG I — A New Foundation

Weak-field scalar sector, Newtonian correspondence, relativistic tests, refractive-index light bending.

QRG II — Full Sourcing Structure and Tensor Modes

Coupling to traceless and anisotropic stress-energy components; emergence of transverse resonance modes; gravitational-wave phenomenology.

QRG III — Quantum Microphysics of the Substrate

Quantum-fluid substrate models, microscopic Hamiltonian, origin of the coupling constant, Lorentz-invariant excitations, dispersion relations.

QRG IV — Cosmology and Large-Scale Resonant Dynamics

Resonant contributions to dark matter and dark energy, FLRW evolution, structure formation, early-universe resonance epochs.
